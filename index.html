OptimusMesh maps a point-cloud input to sparse latent pivots, autoregressively decodes vertices, and then generates triangular faces to obtain a compact render-ready mesh.

Abstract

Generating compact, render-ready, and geometrically faithful 3D meshes directly from point clouds remains a fundamental challenge. Point clouds are unordered and sparse, whereas meshes exhibit irregular structure and inconsistent topology. As a result, most existing approaches avoid direct mesh generation and instead rely on implicit representations followed by surface extraction or reconstruction. Although effective, these pipelines typically produce dense and over-smoothed meshes that ignore geometric features, requiring computationally expensive post-processing and simplification. We present OptimusMesh, a framework for direct compact triangle mesh generation from point clouds using sparse latent pivot conditioning. Our key idea is to extract K=16 sparse latent pivots using a pretrained point-cloud encoder, where each pivot represents a key structural point with learned features that capture the overall geometry of the input shape. Conditioned on these pivots, a Transformer-based two-stage autoregressive decoder sequentially generates mesh vertices and then triangular faces, enabling suppression of output complexity. Sparse latent pivots provide strong global guidance for autoregressive decoding, enabling compact mesh generation without relying on surface extraction, e.g., Marching Cubes, or costly simplification. On a subset of ShapeNet samples, OptimusMesh achieves 55.3% lower CD-L1 than PSR and 58.5% lower than SAP, while producing 97.7% and 99.2% fewer faces, respectively. Compared with recent autoregressive mesh generation methods, our approach further achieves 35.7%--55.4% lower CD-L1 while maintaining highly compact mesh topology and stronger overall geometric fidelity.

Method Overview

OptimusMesh first extracts sparse latent pivots from the input point cloud, then uses the same conditioning to generate vertices and triangle faces autoregressively.

1

Point-cloud input

2048 point-normal samples provide the observed geometry.

2

Sparse latent pivots

The encoder compresses dense observations into 16 learned 48-D pivots.

3

Vertex model

An autoregressive Transformer generates mesh vertices under pivot conditioning.

4

Face model

A second autoregressive Transformer predicts triangle connectivity with a compact face budget.
